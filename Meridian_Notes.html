Meridian Notes

Meridian Notes is a meeting workflow tool for financial advisors. It records a client meeting, generates an AI summary with per-claim confidence flags, converts it into calendared action items, and sends the client a plain-language version they must read, confirm point by point, and sign.

I designed and built the clickable prototype end-to-end — a five-screen flow that validates the core bet: no AI output reaches a client until a human verifies it, and no record counts until the client confirms it.

Challenge

Multiple meetings a day, and every recap is a liability

After each meeting an advisor must reconstruct what was said, extract the numbers that matter, turn commitments into tasks, and send the client a recap — manually, multiple times a day

Reconstructing notes across multiple meetings eats the advisor's evening

Dollar amounts, percentages and dates are exactly where memory errors happen

Recaps are sent, but never verified or acknowledged by the client

Wrong number damages trust

A recap with a wrong figure — even a small one — destroys the very client trust AI was meant to save time building

Verbal advice has no audit trail

Metrics

One north star, and a metric that keeps the gate honest

North star: client confirmation rate — percent of sent summaries the client opens, checks every point, and signs within 7 days. It's the behavior the entire client screen is built to produce

Flag resolution integrity

Do advisors actually edit flagged claims, or click through? Detects whether the accuracy gate is real or theater

Dispute rate

Low-but-nonzero is healthy (clients are reading); rising means summary quality is slipping

Action item completion

Percent of generated tasks completed by their due date — proves the meeting output turns into real work

Reflection

What we'd validate next

The prototype validates the workflow, not the ML. Before investing in real speech-to-text and confidence scoring, four questions decide the roadmap

Riskiest assumptions, in order

  1. Does the accuracy gate produce real verification — or rubber-stamping? Seed summaries with one genuine error and watch 5–8 advisors
  2. Will clients complete per-point confirmation, or check-all in 20 seconds? Pilot with 15–20 real clients
  3. Is the personal hook welcome or creepy? If clients react badly, restrict to CRM-sourced notes only
  4. Can any STT vendor get dollar figures reliably right? If not, V2 leads with in-meeting tap-to-correct instead of post-hoc flags

Trade-offs I'd defend

  • Gated wizard over free navigation — the compliance value depends on the order
  • Blocking gate over advisory warnings
  • Per-point confirmation over one signature
  • Plain-language client copy generated from approved bullets, never independently
  • PIN over full login — with rate limiting and real code delivery in production
